# Validation: InDiD change point detection

Checks `indid()`, `indid_predict()` and `dfr_indid()` against Romanenkova,
Stepikin, Morozov and Zaytsev (2022), *InDiD: Instant disorder detection via
a principled neural network* (ACM Multimedia 2022), and its reference code
(<https://github.com/romanenkova95/InDiD>).

The reference files used are:

* `utils/loss.py`: the InDiD loss;
* `utils/core_models.py`: `BaseRnn`;
* `utils/cpd_models.py`: `CPDModel`;
* `utils/datasets.py`: the SyntheticNormal generator;
* `utils/metrics.py`: the evaluation;
* `train.py`, `test.py` and `configs/`.

```sh
# from the package root
git clone https://github.com/romanenkova95/InDiD data-real/indid/reference
# inside the devcontainer
python3 -I development/validation/indid/prepare_data.py data-real/indid/reference data-real/indid/synthetic
python3 -I development/validation/indid/reference_trace.py data-real/indid/reference data-real/indid/synthetic \
  data-real/indid/trace.json
Rscript development/validation/indid/check_trace.R data-real/indid/trace.json data-real/indid/synthetic
# reference: 30 runs (2 data sets x 3 losses x seeds 102-106), about 1.5 hours on 7 cores
for d in synthetic_1D synthetic_100D; do for l in indid bce combined; do for s in 102 103 104 105 106; do
  python3 -I development/validation/indid/run_reference.py data-real/indid/reference data-real/indid/synthetic \
    development/validation/results/indid/reference $d $l $s; done; done; done
# indid(): the same 30 runs and the comparison, about 1 hour on 7 cores
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 Rscript development/validation/indid/run_indid.R 7 > development/validation/results/indid/run.log
```

`data-real/` is ignored by git. Results go to
`development/validation/results/indid/`.

## Running the reference code here

The container has PyTorch 2.14 and NumPy 2, but not PyTorch Lightning, nor
the video packages imported at the top of the reference files. `ref_env.py`
loads the reference modules unchanged. It adds three things:

* **Stubs:** empty modules replace the missing packages (`av`, `pims`,
  `pytorchvideo`, `ruptures`, `tsai`, and the KL-CPD and TS-CP2 baselines),
  which the SyntheticNormal experiments do not use. `LightningModule` is
  replaced by `torch.nn.Module` with a no-op `log()`.
* **Training loop:** `train_reference()` reproduces `train.py` with Lightning
  1.6: Adam, shuffled batches of 64, one validation pass per epoch, and early
  stopping on `val_loss` with patience 10 and min_delta 0. The validation loss
  is the mean over batches weighted by their size, and the last model is kept.
* **NumPy 2:** `np.trapz` (used by `metrics.py`) is aliased to
  `np.trapezoid`, the same function under the name NumPy 2 uses.

## 1. Step-by-step equivalence (`trace_comparison.csv`)

`reference_trace.py` records the following, and `check_trace.R` replays them
in R:

1. **Losses:** `CPDLoss` (T = 8) and `BCELoss` on random probabilities and
   labels, with their gradients.
2. **Training steps:** three Adam steps per loss from the same initial weights
   (`BaseRnn(1, 4)`, seed 102) on the same batches, in float64.
3. **Float32 steps:** the same steps with the unchanged `CPDModel.training_step`
   (float32).

| check | largest difference |
|---|---|
| InDiD and BCE loss values and gradients (float64) | 1.7e-16 |
| loss, gradients and weights after each Adam step, InDiD and BCE (float64) | 1.3e-15 |
| loss and weights against the unchanged float32 CPDModel | 4e-7 (float32 rounding) |
| probabilities on the test set (float32) | 7e-8 |

In the float64 run, the default dtype is set to float64. `loss.py` builds its
auxiliary tensors with `torch.ones(...)` in the default dtype, so with float32
as the default it rounds the survival products to float32 even for float64
probabilities. That is a difference of order 1e-8.

## 2. Table 2: SyntheticNormal (`table2.csv`, `table2_runs.csv`)

The paper's data are generated by the reference code: 1000 sequences of
length 128, seed 123, split 700/300. Half the sequences change their mean
from 1 to a random mean between 2 and 99; in the test set, 158 of the 300
sequences have a change. The settings are those of the configs:

* one LSTM layer with 4 hidden units (1D) or 8 (100D), T = 32;
* Adam with lr 0.001, batches of 64;
* up to 200 epochs, 100 + 100 for BCE+InDiD;
* early stopping on the test set, as the reference does.

Each method has 5 runs (seeds 102-106). The paper's 5 runs do not give their
seeds.

All three implementations use the evaluation of `metrics.py` with the 102
thresholds of `test.py`. The F1, time to false alarm, delay and covering
reported are those at the threshold with the highest F1, and AUC is the area
under the detection curve. The R port of the metrics (`metrics.R`) gives the
values of `metrics.py` on the probabilities of the 30 reference runs within
9e-5.

| data | loss | AUC indid() | reference | paper | F1 indid() | reference | paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1D | BCE | 625.0 ± 3.9 | 633.8 ± 7.5 | 606.0 ± 4.9 | 0.9904 | 0.9885 | 0.9904 |
| 1D | InDiD | 614.4 ± 20.8 | 609.2 ± 7.3 | 598.7 ± 5.0 | 0.9839 | 0.9878 | 0.9898 |
| 1D | BCE+InDiD | 617.4 ± 9.2 | 618.9 ± 14.8 | 605.3 ± 5.1 | 0.9853 | 0.9859 | 0.9891 |
| 100D | BCE | 602.9 ± 7.2 | 592.3 ± 10.4 | 573.4 ± 0.2 | 0.9911 | 0.9936 | 0.9968 |
| 100D | InDiD | 583.3 ± 11.2 | 578.8 ± 7.1 | 573.5 ± 0.3 | 0.9955 | 0.9943 | 0.9968 |
| 100D | BCE+InDiD | 588.7 ± 13.4 | 578.3 ± 2.0 | 573.3 ± 0.2 | 0.9904 | 0.9911 | 0.9968 |

Time to false alarm (94.2-94.7) and covering (0.991-0.999) are at the level
of the paper for all three. The detection delay of the 1D InDiD runs is
larger for `indid()` (0.94 ± 0.48, against 0.68 ± 0.16 for the reference and
0.54 for the paper).

* **`indid()` against the reference:** the means differ by less than the
  spread between seeds. Of the AUCs, only 1D BCE (8.8) and 100D BCE (10.6)
  differ by more than the larger of the two standard deviations; the F1 values
  differ by at most 0.004.
* **Both against the paper:** both are slightly below the paper on AUC (by
  5-30) and F1 (by up to 0.0064), and the paper's spread is smaller. The paper
  ran torch 1.8 and Lightning 1.6, and its seeds are not given. The InDiD
  loss gives a lower AUC than BCE in 1D in all three, and in 100D in both
  implementations (the paper has them tied in 100D).

## Not reproduced

* Human Activity, MNIST sequences and the video data sets (Explosion, Road
  accidents) need the data on Yandex Disk, and the video models a pretrained
  3D CNN.
* The KL-CPD, TS-CP2 and classic baselines.
